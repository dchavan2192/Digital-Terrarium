# Stationary cue retention pilot

Can weight selection on a supplied recurrent circuit retain a signed cue through five blank ticks? This is a local feasibility study. Each trial starts with zero neural state. A single signed input is presented once, followed by five zeros and one probe. The native brain has seven outputs. Only the turn output is scored. Nonnegative predicts +1, negative predicts −1.

The circuit supplies three weights (bias to turn, cue to turn, and a recurrent turn self-loop). Each seed 6000–6003 starts 16 candidates with independent uniform weights in [−2,2). For 16 replacement generations retain the four best and fill 12 places with children from uniformly chosen elites. Perturb each of the three weights uniformly in [−0.3,0.3), clamping to [−4,4]. Rank by correct count over both cues, then by the worse of the two signed probe responses, then ascending persistent ID. This explicitly rewards the desired mapping across generations.

Save every genotype, parent, ID and training response each generation including founders. Lock the final winner of each seed before diagnostics. For each winner compare hidden and visible probe inputs, each with intact state, one reset immediately before the probe, and clearing each tick. Independent units are the four initial populations. Report founder success separately from searches first reaching success later. Report all four winners and raw probe margins, including ties and failures. The diagnostic pattern of interest is both hidden cues correct intact, one correct after reset, and both visible cues correct after reset. The visible probe presents the cue again. This pilot addresses functional retention in this supplied circuit at the training delay. Navigation, discovery of recurrent topology, delay generalization and ecological benefit require separate experiments.
